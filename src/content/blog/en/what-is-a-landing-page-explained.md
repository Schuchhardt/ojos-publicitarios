If you've heard "you need a landing page" and nodded without being sure what it means, you're not alone. It's a simple idea with a fancy name. Here's what it is, how it works, and when it makes sense for your business.

## What is a landing page?

A landing page is **a single web page designed to get one action from one type of visitor**. That action might be calling you, booking an appointment, requesting a quote, or sending a WhatsApp message.

The name comes from where people "land": after clicking an ad, a link in an email, a post on social media, or a result on Google. Whatever brought them there made a promise. The page's job is to keep that promise and make the next step obvious.

Think of it as a shop window with a single door, rather than a department store with five floors.

## How does a landing page work?

The logic is straightforward:

1. **A visitor arrives** with a specific need ("I want a quote for roof repair").
2. **The page confirms they're in the right place** with a clear headline that matches what they were looking for.
3. **It gives just enough proof and detail** to build trust: what you do, who you do it for, what it looks like, what others say.
4. **It asks for one action** with a visible button or form.

What makes it work is what it leaves out. There's no long menu, no ten different links, no blog. Every extra option is a chance for the visitor to wander off without acting.

## How is a landing page different from a website?

A website is your whole presence: several pages for different questions and different people. A landing page is one page for one purpose.

| | Landing page | Full website |
|---|---|---|
| Pages | One | Several |
| Goal | One action | Inform, build trust, serve many needs |
| Navigation | Minimal or none | Full menu |
| Best for | Offers, campaigns, simple businesses | Businesses with multiple services or lots to explain |

## What should a landing page include?

You don't need much, but each piece has a job:

- **A headline that says what you do and for whom.** "Same-day plumbing repairs in Austin" beats "Welcome to our website."
- **A short supporting line** that adds the main benefit or detail.
- **One primary button.** "Get a quote," "Book a visit," "Message us on WhatsApp." Repeat it further down the page.
- **Proof.** Real photos of your work, real reviews, logos of real clients you've worked with, certifications. Only what's true.
- **A short explanation of the offer.** What's included, how it works, what happens after they contact you.
- **Answers to the usual objections.** Price range if you can share it, timing, service area, guarantees.
- **Contact details that work.** Phone, form and messaging, with the form asking only what you really need.
- **Fast loading and a clean look on a phone.** Most visitors will see it on mobile.

If it doesn't help someone decide, cut it.

## When does a small business need a landing page?

A landing page makes sense in a few common situations:

- **You're just getting online.** If you offer one service or a tight set of them, a good landing page can be your first web presence while you grow.
- **You're running ads.** Sending paid traffic to a general home page wastes money. A page that matches the ad works harder.
- **You're promoting something specific.** A seasonal offer, an event, a new service, a limited booking window.
- **You want to test an idea** before investing in a full site.

It's *not* enough on its own if you have many services that each need explaining, if you sell products online, or if customers expect to browse a lot of information before they contact you. In those cases you'll want a fuller site, and the landing page becomes a complement.

## How do I get people to the page?

A landing page doesn't attract visitors by itself. It converts the ones you send. The usual sources are:

- **Paid ads** (Google, Meta) pointed at the page.
- **Social media** posts and bio links.
- **Email or text campaigns** to existing customers.
- **Search**, if the page is set up well and targets what people actually type.
- **QR codes or links** on printed materials, packaging or in-store signs.

## How much does a landing page cost?

Prices vary widely, so treat these as **rough market ranges**, not quotes:

- **DIY with a website builder:** usually a monthly subscription of roughly $15 to $40, plus your time. Fine for a basic page if you can write clearly and have good photos.
- **Freelancer or small studio:** often somewhere between a few hundred and a few thousand dollars, depending on custom design, copywriting and integrations.
- **Larger agencies:** can go well beyond that, usually because of extra strategy, testing and account management.

What moves the price: custom design versus a template, who writes the copy, photography, forms connected to your calendar or CRM, tracking setup, and how many revisions are included.

Also ask about ongoing costs. Some builders charge a monthly fee for as long as the page exists, and if you stop paying the page can disappear. Make sure the domain and the page itself end up in your name.

## What mistakes make landing pages fail?

- **Too many goals.** Call, subscribe, download, follow us, read the blog. Pick one.
- **A vague headline.** If a stranger can't tell what you do in five seconds, they leave.
- **Stock photos that look generic.** Real photos of your work almost always beat them.
- **Long forms.** Every extra field costs you leads. Ask for name and a way to reach them; get the rest later.
- **No mobile check.** Open it on your phone before anything else.
- **A mismatch with the ad or link.** If the ad promises a discount and the page doesn't mention it, trust drops.
- **No way to measure.** If you don't count calls, forms and messages, you can't improve the page.

## What should I ask before hiring someone to build it?

- Who writes the text, and is it included?
- Will I own the domain, the page and the content?
- Are there monthly fees required to keep it online?
- What happens when a visitor fills in the form? Where does it go?
- How will we know if it's working?
- How long will it take, and how many rounds of changes are included?
- Will it look good and load fast on a phone?
