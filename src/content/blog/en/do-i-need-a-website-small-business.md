Short answer: if you want new customers to find you, you almost certainly need a website. If you're just starting out and your customers already come through word of mouth, you can wait a little. What matters is what you want the site to do and what it costs you not to have one.

## Do I really need a website, or is social media enough?

Social media is enough to *start*. If you're testing an idea, selling to a small circle that already knows you, or taking orders by direct message and it's working, an Instagram or Facebook page can carry you for a while.

The catch: you're building on rented land. The platform sets the rules, the reach and the layout, and it can change any of them without asking you. If an account gets locked or the algorithm shifts, your only way to reach those customers can disappear overnight. **A website is the one place online that belongs to you.**

## What does a website do that social media can't?

- **Appears when people search.** Someone typing a service plus your city into Google is usually ready to buy. A page built for that search can show up; most social posts won't.
- **Answers the same questions once.** Prices or how you price, service area, hours, how to book. You write it once and it works every day.
- **Makes you look established.** Plenty of people check a business online before calling. No site, or a broken one, makes some of them hesitate.
- **Collects leads.** A contact form or booking link turns a visitor into a person you can follow up with, not just a follower.
- **Gives you somewhere to send people.** Ads, flyers, business cards, email signatures and QR codes all need a destination.

## When can you wait on a website?

You can reasonably hold off if:

- All your customers come from referrals and repeat business, and you're already at capacity.
- You're validating an idea and haven't yet proved anyone will pay.
- You sell only through a marketplace that handles listing, payments and reviews for you.
- You have no budget yet, and your time is better spent finding your first ten customers.

Even then, set up a free Google Business Profile if you serve a local area, and keep one page with your contact details somewhere you control. It's a small step that saves headaches later.

## When is a website no longer optional?

Treat it as a priority if any of these sound like you:

- You sell a service people search for: plumbing, accounting, cleaning, design, legal help, repairs.
- You compete with businesses in your area, and they have sites.
- You want customers outside your circle or your city.
- You want bookings, quotes or payments without endless back-and-forth messages.
- You plan to spend money on ads, which need a page that converts.
- You sell to other businesses, which often look you up before replying.
- You serve customers in more than one language or country.

If you ticked two or more, the question is no longer whether, but how soon.

## What is the minimum a small business website needs?

Less than most people think. A simple site, one page or four to six, is enough to start:

- **What you do, for whom and where**, visible on the first screen.
- **One clear action**: call, book, request a quote or buy.
- **Contact details** that are easy to find, with a form that actually reaches you.
- **Real photos** of your work, team or products, not generic ones.
- **Proof**: reviews or examples you really have. Never invent them.
- **A mobile-first layout**, because many visitors will arrive on a phone.
- **Fast loading and a secure connection (HTTPS).**
- **A domain in your name** that you own, like yourbusiness.com.

That's it. A blog, a store or a booking system can come later, when the business needs them. If you want help deciding what shape fits you, that's part of our [web design](/servicios/diseno-web/?lang=en) work.

## How much does a website cost?

These are rough market ranges, and they vary a lot:

- **Do it yourself with a builder:** often around $15 to $40 a month, plus a domain at roughly $10 to $20 a year. Cheap in cash, expensive in your time.
- **A freelancer or small studio for a simple site:** commonly from about $1,000 to $5,000.
- **A custom site with a store, bookings or integrations:** can run well past that, into five figures for complex projects.

The price depends on the number of pages, who writes the copy and takes the photos, and which features you need. Two questions matter more than the sticker price: **who owns the domain and the site?** and **am I locked into a monthly fee just to keep it online?** Some providers keep both in their own name. Ask before you sign.

## Should I start with a free profile and build a website later?

Yes, if you do it in a sensible order:

1. Claim your Google Business Profile and keep your hours, phone number and photos current.
2. Make your social profiles consistent: same name, same photo, same contact info.
3. Build a simple website that all of it points to.
4. Add extras (online booking, a store, automatic replies) once you notice repeated manual work.

A link-in-bio page is not a website. It's a menu of links, and it won't show up when someone searches for what you do.

## What if the website could also save me time?

A site doesn't have to be a brochure. If your day goes to answering the same messages, scheduling by hand or typing up quotes, the site can take over part of that: a booking form that fills your calendar, a quote request that arrives organized, a chat flow for common questions. That's the territory of [custom software and automation](/servicios/desarrollo-de-software/?lang=en). You don't need it on day one, but choose a site that can grow into it.

## What mistakes do owners make?

- **Waiting for it to be perfect.** A clear, simple site live this month beats a perfect one next year.
- **Buying the cheapest option without checking ownership.** You can end up renting your own site.
- **Copying a big company's layout.** A local business needs clarity, not a hero video.
- **Forgetting the phone.** Test every page on yours.
- **Hiding the contact info.** If it takes three clicks to reach you, you lose calls.
- **Never updating it.** Old hours, old prices and dead links cost you trust.
- **Skipping the written part.** Plain words about what you do and who it's for beat slogans.

## What should I ask before hiring someone?

- Who owns the domain, the hosting account and the files when the project ends?
- Is there a mandatory monthly fee, and what does it cover?
- What exactly is included: pages, copy, photos, revisions, mobile version?
- How long will it take, and what do you need from me?
- Can I edit simple things like text, hours and photos myself, or do I have to ask you?
- Can you show work similar to what I need?
- How fast do you reply when something breaks?

Short, clear answers are a good sign. Vague ones are not.
