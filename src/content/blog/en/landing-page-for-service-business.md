If you run a plumbing company, a cleaning service, a law practice, a landscaping crew or any business where people hire you after a call or a quote, your online presence has one job: get that person to contact you. A landing page is built for exactly that.

## What is a landing page for a service business?

It is a single page with one goal. Unlike a full website, it does not try to tell your whole story. It has no long menu, no blog and no five different paths. A visitor arrives, understands what you do, trusts you enough, and takes one action: **call, request a quote or book a time.**

For a service business, that action is the whole point. A beautiful page that nobody calls from is just decoration.

## Do I need a landing page or a full website?

Sometimes both, sometimes just one. A quick way to decide:

- **You run ads or want one service to bring in leads:** use a landing page for that service.
- **You want to show up when people look for your business name, explain several services and look established:** you need a website.
- **You are just starting and have a small budget:** a well-made single page can do the work of a small website for a while.

Many owners end up with a website plus one or two landing pages for the services that make the most money. If you are weighing options, our [web design service](/servicios/diseno-web/?lang=en) covers landing pages, corporate sites and online stores.

## What should be at the top of the page?

The first screen on a phone decides whether someone stays. It needs five things:

1. **A headline that says what you do and where.** For example: "Emergency plumbing in Austin, same-day visits." Clear beats clever.
2. **One supporting line** about who it is for or what makes you different.
3. **A call button** that dials your number when tapped.
4. **A short form or a "Get a quote" button.**
5. **One real photo** of you, your team or your work, not a generic stock image.

If a stranger cannot tell in five seconds what you do and how to reach you, the page is not working.

## What goes below the first screen?

Think of the questions a hesitant customer would ask, in order:

- **What exactly do you do?** List your main services in plain words, with a sentence each.
- **Where do you work?** Name the cities or neighborhoods you cover.
- **Why should I trust you?** Use real reviews, before-and-after photos, licenses, insurance and years in business. Only include what is true and that you can show.
- **How does it work?** Three simple steps: you contact us, we give you a quote, we do the job.
- **What does it cost?** You do not need to publish prices, but you can explain what affects them and how quotes work. This filters out the wrong leads.
- **What if I have a question?** A short FAQ handles the usual objections: timing, guarantees, payment, service area.

Then repeat the call to action at the bottom. Someone who scrolled that far is interested. Do not make them scroll back up.

## What makes people actually contact you?

It is rarely the design. It is usually these small things:

- **Speed.** A slow page on a phone loses visitors before they read a word.
- **Few form fields.** Every extra field lowers the number of people who finish. Name, phone or email, and a short note is usually enough.
- **Real proof.** Specific reviews and photos of finished work beat adjectives like "professional" and "reliable."
- **A clear next step.** Say what happens after they contact you: "We reply within a few hours."
- **Easy contact options.** Some people text, some call. Offer more than one.

## How do people get to the page?

A landing page does not bring traffic by itself. Common sources for service businesses:

- **Google Business Profile.** Link it to your landing page or website so local searches lead somewhere useful.
- **Paid search or social ads.** A page matched to the ad's promise converts better than sending people to a homepage.
- **Social media bios and posts.** Link to the page when you post a specific offer.
- **Print and QR codes.** Vehicle wraps, flyers and door hangers can point to a simple address.

## What should happen after someone fills in the form?

This is where many businesses lose the lead. Someone requests a quote at 9 p.m. and hears nothing until the next afternoon. By then they have called someone else.

At minimum, set up:

- An **instant confirmation** (email or text) so the person knows the request arrived.
- A **notification to you** on your phone, not buried in an inbox.
- A place where leads are **saved**, such as a spreadsheet or simple CRM, so none disappear.

If you get many repeat questions, you can go a step further with automatic replies, booking links or quote forms that feed your system. ## What mistakes should I avoid?

- **Too many goals.** If the page asks people to call, subscribe, follow you and read your blog, they do none of it.
- **Navigation menus** that send visitors away from the page.
- **Vague headlines** like "Welcome to our company."
- **Stock photos** of smiling people in suits when you actually fix roofs.
- **No mobile testing.** Most visitors arrive on a phone. Check that buttons are easy to tap and text is easy to read.
- **Hiding the phone number.** If calls are what you want, make it unmissable.
- **Not tracking results.** At least know how many calls and form submissions came from the page.

## How much does a landing page for a service business cost?

These are rough market ranges, not quotes, and they vary a lot:

- **DIY with a website builder:** about $15 to $50 a month, plus your time. It works if you have a clear message and some patience.
- **Freelancer:** commonly a few hundred to a few thousand dollars, depending on experience, copywriting and design.
- **Studio or agency:** usually more, often justified when you need strategy, copy, custom design and integrations together.

What changes the price: whether someone writes the copy, whether you need photos, how many pages or versions you need, forms and integrations, and who owns the result.

That last point matters. Ask whether the page will be **in your name**, on your own domain and hosting, with no obligation to keep paying a monthly fee just to keep it online.

## What should I ask before hiring someone?

- Can you show landing pages you built for service businesses, and what they were meant to achieve?
- Who writes the copy, and how many rounds of changes are included?
- Will the page load fast and work well on a phone?
- How will form submissions and calls be tracked?
- Will the domain, hosting and files be in my name?
- What happens after launch if I want to change a headline or a photo?
- How fast do you reply when I have a question?

Honest answers, and a clear scope in writing, matter more than a long portfolio.

## A simple checklist before you publish

- The headline says what you do and where.
- There is a tap-to-call button on mobile.
- The form is short and works. Test it yourself.
- You have real photos and at least a few real reviews.
- Your service area is listed.
- The page loads quickly on a phone.
- You get notified the moment a lead arrives.
- You know how you will reply and how fast.
