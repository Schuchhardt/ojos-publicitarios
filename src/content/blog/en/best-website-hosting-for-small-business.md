There is no single best hosting company for every small business. The best choice depends on what your site does: a five-page brochure, a booking site, an online store, or something with custom software behind it.

This guide explains what hosting is, which type fits which business, what it roughly costs, and what to check before you pay.

## What does website hosting actually do?

Your website is a set of files and, often, a database. **Hosting is the computer that stores them and delivers them to visitors** every time someone types your address or clicks your link.

Three things get mixed up all the time:

- **Domain:** your address, like yourbusiness.com. You rent it yearly.
- **Hosting:** where the site lives. You pay monthly or yearly.
- **The website itself:** the design, text and features.

You need all three. They can come from one company or from different ones.

## What types of hosting exist?

Here are the main options, in plain language:

- **Website builder hosting:** included in the plan of a drag-and-drop builder. Easy, but you rent a closed system.
- **Shared hosting:** your site shares a server with many others. Cheap and fine for small, low-traffic sites.
- **Managed hosting:** the provider handles updates, security and backups for a specific platform. More expensive, less maintenance for you.
- **VPS or cloud hosting:** a slice of a server with guaranteed resources. Good for stores, busy sites and custom software.
- **Modern static or app hosting:** common for sites built with current development tools. Fast and often inexpensive, but it assumes a developer is involved.

## Which hosting fits which kind of business?

**A simple brochure site** (services, contact, a few pages): shared hosting or a builder plan is usually enough. Don't overbuy.

**A site that gets leads and changes often:** managed hosting is worth considering, mainly for automatic backups and support.

**An online store:** pick hosting that can handle more traffic and checkout without slowing down. If you use a store platform that includes hosting, that part is covered. If you self-host a store, budget for something stronger than the cheapest plan.

**A site with custom software** (booking, quotes, integrations): you need hosting that supports the technology it was built with. Your developer should tell you what, not the other way around.

## How much does website hosting cost?

Treat these as broad, approximate market ranges. They change by provider and by year:

- **Basic shared hosting:** from a few dollars up to roughly $25 a month.
- **Managed hosting:** often around $20 to $60 a month, sometimes more.
- **VPS or cloud for stores and custom apps:** commonly from about $20 to $100 or more a month.
- **Domain:** typically a modest yearly fee.

The trap is the **introductory price**. Many plans are cheap for the first term and noticeably more expensive at renewal. Always ask what you pay in year two.

## What should you check before buying?

Use this list when comparing options:

1. **Backups:** are they automatic, how often, and can you restore them yourself?
2. **SSL certificate:** the padlock in the browser should be included.
3. **Uptime and speed:** look for a clear uptime commitment and servers reasonably close to your customers.
4. **Support:** real people, in your language, with a response time you can live with.
5. **Renewal price:** the actual price after the first term.
6. **Business email:** whether it's included or billed separately.
7. **Room to grow:** can you upgrade without rebuilding the site?
8. **Exit options:** can you export your site and move it elsewhere?

That last point is the one owners forget. If leaving is painful, the provider has leverage over you.

## Who should own the hosting account?

**You should.** The domain, the hosting and the site files should all be in your name, with you as the main contact.

A common problem: a freelancer or agency sets everything up under their own account, then disappears or raises prices, and the owner can't get in. Before starting any project, ask directly: *Whose name will the domain and hosting be under, and will I get all the logins?*

## What mistakes do small businesses make with hosting?

- **Buying the cheapest plan without reading the renewal terms.**
- **Overbuying:** paying for a powerful server when the site is five pages.
- **Underbuying for a store:** a plan that slows down when customers check out costs you sales.
- **No backups,** or backups stored only on the same server.
- **Letting someone else hold the account.**
- **Hosting in one place, domain in another, and nobody knows where.** Keep a simple document with every login and renewal date.
- **Choosing hosting before choosing the site.** Decide what the site must do first, then match the hosting.

## What should you ask the person building your site?

If someone else is handling this, ask:

- Which hosting do you recommend for my site, and why?
- What will it cost me per month and per year, including renewals?
- Will the hosting and domain be in my name?
- How are backups handled, and who restores the site if something breaks?
- If I want to move later, can I take everything with me?
- Who handles security updates, and is that included in the price?
