Most owners don't fail at social media because they picked the wrong app. They fail because they post without a goal, burn out after a few weeks, and never check whether anything came of it. Here is a practical way to do it, whether you run it yourself or hand it off.

## What does social media marketing mean for a small business?

It means using your social accounts to get **one specific business result**: more phone calls, more bookings, more store visits, more quote requests. It does not mean "being present," and it does not mean collecting likes.

For a small business, social media usually does three jobs:

- **Shows you're real and trustworthy:** photos of actual work, actual people, actual answers.
- **Answers questions before customers ask them:** pricing logic, how long things take, what's included.
- **Sends interested people to a next step:** a call, a message, a booking page or your website.

If a post doesn't help with one of those, it's probably filler.

## What should you decide before you post anything?

Write these three things on a single page. It takes 20 minutes and saves months of guessing.

1. **The goal.** Pick one. For example: "get 10 quote requests a month" or "fill Tuesday and Wednesday appointments." Only one, at least at the start.
2. **The customer.** Who exactly are you talking to? A homeowner who needs a remodel is different from a restaurant owner who needs a supplier. Describe one real person.
3. **The next step.** What should someone do after seeing your post? Call, send a message, book, visit the site. Every profile and most posts should point to it.

Also check the **destination**. If someone taps the link in your bio and lands on an outdated page, or nothing at all, you lose them. You don't need anything fancy, but you do need a clear page with your services, your area and a way to contact you. If yours is missing or old, a simple [web design project](/servicios/diseno-web/?lang=en) usually pays for itself in leads you'd otherwise lose.

## Which platforms should you use?

Start with **one or two**. Not five. Here is a rough guide:

- **Facebook:** good for local services, community groups, events and customers who are older or want to ask a quick question.
- **Instagram:** good for visual businesses: food, beauty, fitness, home projects, retail.
- **Short video (Reels, TikTok, YouTube Shorts):** good if you can show a process or a before-and-after. One video can often be reused across all three.
- **LinkedIn:** good for business-to-business services and professional work.
- **Google Business Profile:** not social media in the strict sense, but if you serve a local area, keep it updated with photos and hours.

The rule: **go where your customers already spend time and where you can realistically keep up.** If you're not sure, ask your last ten customers how they found you. Their answers beat any trend article.

## What should you post?

Mix a few types of content so you don't sound like a constant ad:

- **Proof:** finished jobs, before-and-after photos, happy customers (with their permission).
- **Answers:** the questions you hear every week. "How long does it take?" "What's the difference between X and Y?"
- **Behind the scenes:** how you make or do the work, your team, your day.
- **Offers and reminders:** a new service, open slots, a seasonal deadline.
- **Local touches:** events you attend, other businesses you work with, neighborhood news.

A workable rule of thumb: about three out of four posts should be useful or interesting on their own, and one can ask directly for the sale.

A few practical tips:

- Phone photos and videos are fine if the light is good and the shot is steady.
- Show faces. People hire people.
- Write the way you talk.
- One idea and one next step per post.

## How often do you need to post?

Less than you think, but **more steadily than you'd like**. Two or three posts a week on one platform, kept up for months, beats daily posting for two weeks followed by silence.

Pick a number you can still hit in a busy month. You can always raise it later.

## What does a simple weekly routine look like?

Batching makes this manageable:

- **Once a month (1 to 2 hours):** list 8 to 12 post ideas from customer questions, recent jobs and upcoming dates.
- **Once a week (about 1 hour):** take the photos and videos, write the captions and schedule them using each platform's built-in scheduler.
- **Every day (about 10 minutes):** answer comments and direct messages.

That last one matters more than people expect. **Messages are leads.** A question left unanswered for two days is usually a customer who went to someone else.

## How do you know if it's working?

Track a few numbers once a month, in a simple spreadsheet:

- **Leads from social:** messages, calls, form submissions and bookings. Add "How did you hear about us?" to your intake, whether by phone, form or in person.
- **Clicks to your website or booking page:** the platform's own insights show this.
- **Which posts get saved, shared or trigger messages:** do more of those.

Followers are the least useful number. A small account that brings in five customers a month is doing better than a big one that brings none.

Give it **two to three months** before you judge. Early on, you're looking for signals: more questions, more messages, more people mentioning a post.

### What about paid ads?

Once you know what content gets a response, a small paid budget can amplify it. Keep it simple: one clear offer, a local audience, a specific next step. Don't pay to boost a post that has no clear action attached.

## Should you do it yourself or hire someone?

**Do it yourself if:** your business is simple, you enjoy it, you can set aside a few hours a week, and you're still figuring out what works.

**Hire help if:**

- You've tried and stopped more than once.
- You're too busy to keep a schedule.
- You want strategy, content, ads and reporting handled together.
- Your brand's look and tone matter a lot.

If you hire, ask these questions:

- **Who creates the content,** and how do you get photos and video from my business?
- **Will the accounts stay in my name,** and will I have admin access? The answer should be yes, always.
- **What exactly do I get each month:** number of posts, replies, reports?
- **How will we measure results,** and does it tie back to my goal?
- **What's the contract length,** and how do I leave if it's not working?
- **Who answers comments and messages,** and how fast?

## What mistakes should you avoid?

- **Posting with no goal.** You can't tell if it's working.
- **Being on five platforms.** You'll do all of them badly.
- **Buying followers.** They never buy anything.
- **Only posting promotions.** People tune out.
- **Ignoring messages.** It's the fastest way to waste your effort.
- **Letting someone else own your accounts.** If the relationship ends, you lose your audience.
- **Copying big brands.** Their budget and audience aren't yours.
- **Quitting at week four.** That's usually right before it starts to build.
