Every small business owner gets pitched the same list: SEO, ads, social media, email, influencers, AI tools. All of them can work. Most of them are a waste if you pick them in the wrong order.

This guide helps you choose **what to do first, what to add later, and what to skip** based on how your business actually gets customers.

## What counts as digital marketing for a small business?

Digital marketing is anything that helps people find you, trust you, and contact you online. In practice, it comes down to six pieces:

- **Your website:** the place where everything else sends people.
- **Local search:** your Google Business Profile and reviews.
- **SEO:** showing up when people search for what you sell.
- **Paid ads:** search or social ads you pay for per click or per view.
- **Email and text messages:** staying in touch with people who already know you.
- **Social media:** posting content and talking to your audience.

They are not equal. The first two are the foundation. The rest are amplifiers.

## What should I have in place before spending on marketing?

Marketing sends people somewhere. If that place is weak, you pay to lose them. Before you spend, check four things:

1. **A website that explains what you do in five seconds.** What you offer, where you work, and how to contact you, visible on a phone. If yours is old or confusing, fix that first. [Web design](/servicios/diseno-web/?lang=en) is usually the best first marketing investment.
2. **A complete Google Business Profile** (if you serve a local area): correct hours, photos, services, and a link to your site.
3. **One clear way to contact you:** a form, a phone number, a booking link. Not five different ones.
4. **A way to answer quickly.** A lead that waits two days is often gone.

If any of these is missing, spending on ads is like pouring water into a leaky bucket.

## Which channel is best for my type of business?

The answer depends on how people buy from you. Here are three common cases.

**Local service business** (plumber, dentist, salon, accountant, landscaper): people search when they need you. Prioritize your Google Business Profile, reviews, a simple service website, and search ads for urgent keywords. Social media is secondary.

**Online store or product business:** people compare and browse. Prioritize a fast, clear store, product photos, email for repeat customers, and ads that show products. Social media matters more here, especially if your product looks good in photos or video.

**B2B or high-ticket services** (consulting, manufacturing, software, contractors): buyers research for weeks. Prioritize a credible website with clear services and proof of your work, SEO around the problems you solve, and email follow-up. LinkedIn can help if your buyers are there.

Notice what repeats: **the website and local search come first in every case.**

## Is SEO worth it for a small business?

Usually yes, but with the right expectations. SEO means making your site easy for search engines to understand and useful for the people searching. It is slow, often several months before you see steady results, but it does not stop when you stop paying.

For a small business, focus on the basics:

- A page for each main service, written in the words your customers use.
- Your city or area mentioned naturally where it applies.
- Fast loading and good mobile display.
- Reviews and a consistent name, address, and phone number across the web.

Be wary of anyone who guarantees a number one ranking. Nobody controls Google.

## Are paid ads worth it?

Paid ads are the fastest way to get visits, and the easiest way to burn money. They work best when:

- You already know what a customer is worth to you.
- Your website or landing page is clear and focused on one action.
- You can answer leads quickly.

Start small, with one campaign aimed at one service or product, and watch it for a few weeks before increasing the budget. Ask for access to the ad account in your own name so you can see exactly where the money goes.

## What about social media and email?

**Social media** builds familiarity and trust. It works well when your audience is already there and you can post consistently. It works poorly when you post sporadically on five platforms. Choose one or two and do them properly. If you want help with this, see [digital marketing and social media management](/servicios/marketing-digital/?lang=en).

**Email and text messages** are underrated. They reach people who already know you, cost little, and bring repeat business. Even a short monthly message to past customers can pay off more than another ad campaign. The key is collecting contacts with permission from day one.

## How much should I expect to spend?

There is no honest universal number, but rough market ranges help you plan:

- **Ad budgets:** many small businesses start somewhere between a few hundred and a couple of thousand dollars a month in ad spend, separate from any management fee.
- **Agency or freelancer management:** roughly from a few hundred to a few thousand dollars a month, depending on scope.
- **SEO:** often a monthly retainer in a similar range, or a one-time setup for a small site.

These are approximate and vary widely by industry and city. What matters more than the number is that **you know what you are buying and what result you are measuring.**

## What mistakes should I avoid?

- **Doing everything at once.** Three channels done well beat seven done badly.
- **Skipping the website.** Ads and social posts that lead to a weak site waste money.
- **Not tracking calls and forms.** If you cannot tell where leads come from, you cannot decide what to keep.
- **Paying for followers or vanity numbers.** Likes do not pay bills; inquiries and sales do.
- **Letting someone else own your accounts.** Your website, ad accounts, and social profiles should be in your name.
- **Expecting overnight results from SEO** or giving up on ads after one week.

## What should I ask before hiring someone for digital marketing?

- Which channels would you start with for my business, and why?
- What will I see each month, and how will we know it is working?
- Who owns the accounts, the website, and the data?
- Is there a long contract, and how do I leave if it is not working?
- Can you show me work similar to what I need, and explain what you did?
- How fast do you respond when I write?

A good answer is specific and a little cautious. A bad one is a list of guarantees.

## A simple order to follow

If you want a plan you can start this month:

1. Fix or build a clear website.
2. Complete your Google Business Profile and ask happy customers for reviews.
3. Set up one way to capture contacts and respond fast.
4. Run one small ad campaign or start basic SEO, not both at full scale.
5. Add one social platform and an occasional email.
6. Review monthly and keep only what brings inquiries.
