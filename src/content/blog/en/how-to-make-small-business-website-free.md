Yes, you can. But free almost always comes with a catch, and it's better to know what it is before you put your business name on a page you don't fully control.

## Can you really make a website for a small business for free?

Yes. Many website builders let you publish a basic site at no cost. Other free tools, like a Google Business Profile or a social media page, can also work as a minimal web presence.

What you get is a **starter site**, not a finished business asset. Free plans exist so that you'll upgrade later. Treat them as a trial or a temporary stopgap, and you won't be disappointed.

## What do you give up with a free website?

The exact limits vary by platform, so check the current terms. The usual trade-offs look like this:

- **A borrowed web address.** Instead of yourbusiness.com, you get something like yourbusiness.platform.com. It looks less trustworthy, it's harder to remember, and if you move to another platform, you lose it.
- **The platform's ads or branding.** A banner or footer promoting the builder, right on your page.
- **Limited features.** Few pages, little storage, restricted forms, and often no online payments or booking.
- **No business email.** An address like info@yourbusiness.com generally needs your own domain.
- **Little control over search visibility.** Limited options for page titles, URLs, and speed.
- **Platform dependence.** If they change the terms, raise prices, or retire the free plan, you adapt or start over.

None of that is fatal for a hobby. For a business that wants phone calls and quote requests, it matters.

## What are your free options?

1. **A free plan from a website builder.** Best for a simple one-page site with your services and a contact button.
2. **A Google Business Profile.** A free listing that shows your hours, phone number, photos, and reviews on Google Search and Maps. If you serve customers locally, it's often the most useful free thing you can set up. It isn't a website, but it does a lot of the same job.
3. **A social media page.** Fast to set up, and your customers may already be there. But you're renting that audience, and you can't control how the page appears or is found.
4. **A link-in-bio page.** Works as a stopgap: a single page with your phone, WhatsApp, and a few links.
5. **An AI site generator.** It can give you a first draft in minutes. You still need to check every sentence for accuracy, and the free versions come with the same limits as other free plans.

The best free setup for most local businesses is a **one-page site plus a complete Google Business Profile**, with both pointing to each other.

## What does free end up costing?

Most people who start free end up paying a little. Here's what to expect, as rough market figures:

- **Your own domain:** roughly $10 to $20 per year for common extensions.
- **A paid plan** to connect your domain and remove platform ads: often somewhere around $10 to $40 per month, depending on the builder and features.
- **Business email:** usually a small monthly fee per user.
- **Your time:** a first site easily takes a weekend or more, and time spent on a site is time not spent on customers.
- **The cost of looking unfinished:** a site with ads, a strange address, and no photos can make a good business look careless.

A realistic low-cost setup (domain plus an entry-level paid plan) can run a few hundred dollars a year. That's approximate, but it's a better benchmark than zero.

## How do you build a free site step by step?

1. **Pick one goal.** Phone calls, quote requests, bookings, or store visits. A small site that does one thing well beats a big one that does five things poorly.
2. **Write the content first.** What you do, who it's for, where you work, how to contact you, and what makes you different. Use plain language.
3. **Choose a builder and read what the free plan excludes.** Look for the address format, ads, page limits, and whether forms work.
4. **Keep it to one page at first.** A clear headline, your services, a few real reviews from real customers, your phone number, address or service area, hours, and a contact button.
5. **Use your own photos.** Real photos of your work or your team beat generic images.
6. **Check it on a phone.** Most visitors will see it there.
7. **Connect it to your other profiles.** Link it from your Google Business Profile and social accounts.
8. **Run a simple test.** Ask someone who doesn't know your business to find your phone number and what you offer in 10 seconds.

## When is free enough, and when should you pay?

**Free is fine when:**

- You're just starting and still validating the idea.
- Your customers come mostly by word of mouth.
- You need something live this week as a placeholder.

**Pay when:**

- People search for your services on Google.
- You want business email, online payments, or bookings.
- You're sending ads or social traffic to the site.
- The site is your main way of getting customers.
- You hesitate to share the link because of how it looks.

When you outgrow free, you have two paths: a paid builder plan, which is cheaper upfront but is a recurring cost, or a site built for you. ## What mistakes should you avoid?

- **Building the site on someone else's account.** If a nephew, friend, or freelancer sets it up under their email, you may not be able to get it back. You should own the domain and the account.
- **Hiding your contact information.** Phone, email, and location should be visible without scrolling or searching.
- **Too many pages.** Five thin pages are worse than one clear one.
- **Publishing unedited AI or template text.** Customers can tell, and it may say things that aren't true about your business.
- **Forgetting renewals.** If your domain expires, someone else can buy it.
- **Never updating it.** Old hours, old prices, and a broken form cost you customers without you noticing.

## What should you ask before handing it to someone?

If you decide to pay someone to build it, ask:

- **Who owns the domain and the accounts?** The answer should be you.
- **What do I pay after launch?** Hosting, platform fees, maintenance, and support.
- **Can I take my site with me?** If you leave, can you move it?
- **Who updates it?** And how fast do they reply?
- **What's included?** Mobile design, basic search setup, contact forms, and Google profile connection.
- **What happens if I want to add booking, payments, or automations later?**

Anyone who answers these clearly is probably someone you can work with.
