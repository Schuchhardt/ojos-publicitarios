If you're asking this, you probably want a number before you talk to anyone. Here it is, along with the things that move it up or down.

## How much does a social media manager cost for a small business?

Most small businesses in the U.S. pay **roughly $500 to $2,500 a month** for a freelancer and **roughly $1,000 to $5,000 or more a month** for an agency. These are broad, approximate market ranges, not quotes. Where you land depends on how many platforms you use, how often you post, and how much content someone has to create from scratch.

A quick map of the options:

- **Freelancer, part-time:** about $500 to $2,500 per month, usually for a fixed number of posts on one to three platforms.
- **Small agency or studio:** about $1,000 to $5,000+ per month, typically with strategy, design, reporting and more than one person involved.
- **Hourly consultant:** about $25 to $100 per hour. Good for an audit, training or a one-time plan, not for daily posting.
- **Part-time employee:** often $1,500 to $3,500 per month in pay for 15 to 20 hours a week, plus payroll taxes and the time you spend managing them.
- **Full-time employee:** often $45,000 to $70,000 a year in salary alone. Rarely makes sense for a small business that is just getting started online.

If a quote is far below these ranges, ask exactly what is being delivered. If it's far above, ask what you're getting that you couldn't get for less.

## What makes the price go up or down?

Two businesses can both say they want social media management and need very different amounts of work. These are the factors that change the price:

- **Number of platforms.** Instagram alone is one job. Instagram, Facebook, TikTok and LinkedIn is four jobs, because each one has its own format and audience.
- **Posting frequency.** Three posts a week and daily posting are not the same workload.
- **Content creation.** Writing captions over photos you provide costs less than designing graphics, editing short videos or planning shoots.
- **Community management.** Answering comments and direct messages takes real time, and it's often where the sales happen.
- **Strategy and reporting.** A monthly plan and a clear report cost more than simply publishing.
- **Approvals and industry.** Regulated or technical industries need more review, and more review means more hours.

The cheapest packages usually cover only scheduling and captions. That's fine if that's all you need, but know what you're buying.

## What should a monthly package include?

A reasonable package for a small business spells out these items in writing:

- A **posting schedule** (for example, a set number of posts per week per platform).
- A **content calendar** you can see before things go live.
- **Design or video work**, with the number of pieces stated.
- **Caption writing** in your brand's voice.
- **Replying to comments and messages**, with a clear response window.
- A **monthly report** that ties the numbers to your goals, such as calls, messages or visits to your site, not just likes.
- **Access arrangements**: the accounts stay in your name and you keep the logins.

If a proposal says something vague like full social media management, ask them to list what that means in posts, pieces and hours.

## What does the price usually not include?

Budget for extras so the first invoice doesn't surprise you:

- **Ad spend.** Paid promotion is separate from the manager's fee. Some managers also charge for setting up and optimizing campaigns.
- **Tools.** Scheduling, design or analytics software may be included or billed on top. Ask who pays and whose name the accounts are under.
- **Photo and video shoots.** Original footage of your products, staff or location usually costs more than editing what you already have.
- **Influencer or creator fees**, if you use them.
- **Stock images or music licenses.**

## Freelancer, agency or in-house: which one fits?

**Choose a freelancer** if you need steady posting on one or two platforms and you're comfortable giving feedback. It's the most affordable route, but you rely on one person. If they get sick or disappear, your content stops.

**Choose an agency or studio** if you need strategy, design and video together, or if social media connects with your website, ads and email. It costs more, but you get a team and continuity. Our [digital marketing and social media service](/servicios/marketing-digital/?lang=en) works this way, with content, campaigns and SEO planned together.

**Hire in-house** when social media is central to your business and you have enough work to keep someone busy. For most small businesses, that comes later.

A common path: start with a freelancer or small studio for three to six months, see what works, then decide whether to scale up.

## How do I decide on a budget?

Start from the goal, not from the price list.

1. **Pick the platform where your customers already are.** A local restaurant and a B2B consultancy rarely need the same one.
2. **Define one result that matters.** More messages, more bookings, more foot traffic. Not just followers.
3. **Start small and consistent.** One or two platforms done well beat four done poorly.
4. **Commit to a trial period.** Three months is a fair window to judge a content strategy. A single month rarely tells you much.
5. **Set aside a separate ad budget** if you want faster reach, and decide it independently from the management fee.

If your budget is under a few hundred dollars a month, consider doing the posting yourself with a simple weekly routine, and pay a professional for a one-time strategy and templates instead.

## What red flags should I watch for?

- **Guaranteed followers or viral results.** No one can promise that honestly. Bought followers also hurt more than they help.
- **Accounts created under their name.** You should own every login and every account.
- **Long lock-in contracts** with no way out if the work is poor.
- **Vague reporting**, or reports that only show likes and reach.
- **No questions about your business.** A good manager asks about your customers, your margins and what a good month looks like before quoting.
- **A price that is far below the market** with a long list of promises.

## What should I ask before hiring someone?

- How many posts, designs and videos are included each month?
- Who creates the content, and who approves it before it goes live?
- How fast do you reply to comments and messages, and who handles them?
- What will the monthly report show, and how does it connect to my goals?
- Will the accounts and tools be in my name?
- What happens if I want to stop? Is there a minimum term?
- What is not included in the fee?

A clear, specific answer to each is a better sign than a polished portfolio.

## Can some of the work be automated?

Some of it, yes. Scheduling posts, capturing leads from messages and answering the same basic questions can be handled by simple tools, which frees up the human work for content and relationships.
