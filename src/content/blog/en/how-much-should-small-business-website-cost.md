If you're putting your business online for the first time, the quotes you collect can look absurd: one person says $300, another says $12,000, for what sounds like the same thing. Both can be honest. They are usually pricing different things.

This guide gives you rough market ranges in USD, explains what moves the price, and lists what to check before you sign anything.

## How much should a small business website cost?

For a typical small business, with a handful of pages, a contact form, a design that works on phones and basic search setup, a reasonable budget is **roughly $1,000 to $8,000 up front**. Add a few hundred dollars a year to keep it online.

Below about $500, you are mostly paying for a template that you fill in yourself. Above $10,000, you should be getting something specific: custom features, a lot of content, or connections to other systems.

Treat every number in this article as approximate. Prices vary by city, by provider and above all by scope.

## What are the typical price ranges?

Here is how the market tends to break down:

- **DIY with a website builder:** about $0 to $500 upfront, then roughly $15 to $60 per month in platform fees. The real cost is your time.
- **Freelancer using a template:** roughly $500 to $3,000, depending on how much they customize.
- **Small studio or agency, custom design:** roughly $3,000 to $10,000 for a business site.
- **Online store:** roughly $3,000 to $25,000 or more, depending on the number of products, payments, shipping rules and integrations.
- **Custom functionality** such as online booking, instant quotes or a customer portal: priced more like software, often $5,000 or more on top of the site.

A single landing page costs less than a full multi-page site, and a site that sells costs more than one that only informs. That alone explains a lot of the gap between quotes.

## What makes the price go up or down?

These are the factors that actually change a quote:

- **Number of pages.** Five pages and thirty pages are different projects.
- **Template or custom design.** A template is faster and cheaper. Custom design takes more hours but fits your brand and your customers.
- **Content.** Who writes the text and takes or edits the photos? If the provider does it, the price rises. If you bring it ready, it falls.
- **Features.** Booking, payments, a store, a members area, multiple languages.
- **Integrations.** Connecting the site to your calendar, CRM, email tool or messaging.
- **Search setup.** Basic setup is common. A full content and SEO strategy is a separate cost.
- **Timeline.** Rushing a project usually costs more.
- **Ownership.** Who owns the site, the domain and the accounts afterward.

When two quotes differ by a lot, compare this list line by line before deciding which one is the bargain.

## What costs come after the website launches?

This is where many owners get surprised. Budget for:

- **Domain:** roughly $10 to $25 per year.
- **Hosting:** roughly $5 to $50 per month for a typical small business site, more for busy stores.
- **Business email:** usually a few dollars per user per month.
- **Maintenance:** updates, backups and security, either by you or by someone you pay.
- **Content changes:** new services, new prices, new photos. Ask whether you can edit them yourself.
- **Store fees:** online stores pay payment processing on each sale, often around 3% plus a small fixed fee.

Also ask about **subscriptions**. Some providers charge a monthly fee indefinitely for a site you never actually own. That can cost more over three years than a one-time build. A good arrangement is simple: you pay for the work, and the site and domain are in your name.

## Is a cheap website a bad idea?

Not always. A cheap site is fine when you are testing a business idea, when you only need a simple page to be found and contacted, or when you enjoy tinkering.

Cheap becomes expensive when:

- You rebuild it within a year because it can't grow with you.
- It looks broken on phones, where most visitors arrive.
- You can't edit anything without paying again.
- The domain or the account is in someone else's name.
- It never brings a customer because nobody thought about what visitors need to see.

The question is not whether it's cheap. It's whether it does the job you need.

## How do you set a budget for your website?

Follow these steps before you ask anyone for a price:

1. **Define the goal in one sentence.** For example: get quote requests from local homeowners, or sell 20 products a month online.
2. **List the pages you need.** Home, services, about, contact, and maybe a gallery or store.
3. **Decide what you want to edit yourself.** Prices, photos, blog posts.
4. **Note any must-have features.** Booking, payments, a second language.
5. **Pick a range using the market numbers above.** Then ask two or three providers to quote the same brief, so you compare like with like.

If you also answer the same questions or schedule appointments by hand, mention it. Sometimes part of that work can be handled by a form or a simple [custom software and automation](/servicios/desarrollo-de-software/?lang=en) setup connected to the site, and it is cheaper to plan it from the start than to bolt it on later.

## What should a quote include?

A useful quote is specific. Look for:

- The **number of pages** and what goes on each.
- Whether the design is **custom or template-based**.
- Who provides **text and images**.
- Features and **integrations** included.
- **Revisions**: how many rounds and what counts as a change.
- **Timeline** and what you must deliver, and when.
- What is **not** included.
- **Ownership** of the site, domain, hosting account and design files.
- Ongoing costs, written out.

A one-line quote that says only website, $2,000 leaves too much to argue about later.

## What should you ask before hiring someone?

- Can I see two or three similar sites you have built?
- Will the site, domain and hosting be in my name?
- Do I need to pay a monthly fee to keep the site running? What for?
- How will the site look and work on a phone?
- Can I edit text and photos myself, and will you show me how?
- What happens if I want changes after launch?
- How fast do you respond when something breaks?
- Who handles basic search setup?

Honest providers answer these plainly. Be cautious if answers are vague or if they dodge the ownership question.

## What are the most common budgeting mistakes?

- **Comparing prices without comparing scope.** A $1,500 quote and a $6,000 quote may not include the same things.
- **Forgetting content.** The best design fails if the text and photos are weak or late.
- **Ignoring ongoing costs.** Hosting, email, maintenance and fees add up.
- **Paying for features you won't use.** Start with what brings customers, then grow.
- **Not owning the assets.** Moving a site you don't own is slow, costly, and sometimes impossible.
- **Treating the site as finished at launch.** Set aside a small yearly amount for updates.
